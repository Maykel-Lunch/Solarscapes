Famous Astronomers

Galileo Galilei (1564-1642)

Pioneer of observational astronomy. Galileo revolutionized astronomy by using the telescope to observe Jupiter's moons, Venus phases, and sunspots. His work challenged geocentric models and supported heliocentrism.

Isaac Newton (1642-1727)

Formulated the laws of motion and universal gravitation. Newton's theories provided the mathematical framework that explained planetary orbits and became the foundation of classical mechanics used in astronomy.

Edwin Hubble (1889-1953)

Discovered that galaxies exist beyond the Milky Way and that the universe is expanding. Hubble's findings revolutionized cosmology and demonstrated the vast scale of the universe far beyond what was previously believed.

Carl Sagan (1934-1996)

Renowned astronomer, cosmologist, and science communicator. Sagan popularized astronomy and made complex concepts accessible. His Voyager Golden Record and public outreach inspired generations to study astronomy.

Johannes Kepler (1571-1630)

Formulated the three laws of planetary motion, describing the elliptical orbits of planets around the Sun. Kepler's work provided crucial evidence for heliocentrism and laid the groundwork for Newton's theory of gravitation.

Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin (1900-1979)

Discovered that stars are composed primarily of hydrogen and helium. Her groundbreaking thesis "Stellar Atmospheres" revolutionized astrophysics and demonstrated that the Sun's composition is similar to other stars.
